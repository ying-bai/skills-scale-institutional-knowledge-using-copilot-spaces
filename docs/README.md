# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This README is the entry point for the OctoAcme project management documentation set. It provides a quick summary of the project management approach and links to the detailed guides used across the delivery lifecycle.

## Project Management Process Summary

OctoAcme uses a lightweight but structured project management approach that spans initiation, planning, execution, release, and retrospective improvement. Work starts by validating the business need, defining measurable success criteria, identifying stakeholders, and confirming whether an initiative should move forward. Once approved, teams turn the work into an actionable plan by breaking it into shippable increments, prioritizing backlog items, defining acceptance criteria, estimating scope, and identifying dependencies, risks, milestones, and release expectations.

Roles are defined to support cross-functional collaboration. Project Managers coordinate delivery, timelines, risks, communications, and documentation. Product Managers define outcomes, prioritize work, and measure customer and business impact. Developers build, test, review, and help estimate work while surfacing technical risks. QA contributors validate quality and acceptance criteria, and stakeholders provide direction, approvals, and feedback.

Execution and communication are managed through regular team rhythms and shared artifacts. OctoAcme emphasizes daily or twice-weekly standups, weekly PM and product syncs, delivery reviews, sprint or milestone demos, and monthly stakeholder updates when needed. Teams use project boards as a source of truth for workflow status, track risks in a risk register, and escalate blockers through a defined path from team-level triage to PM, Product Lead, and Sponsor.

Quality assurance is embedded throughout execution and release. OctoAcme expects unit tests for new logic, integration tests where applicable, end-to-end smoke tests for critical flows, CI-based linting and security scanning, and manual QA when needed for feature acceptance. Pull requests should stay small when possible, include issue links and acceptance criteria, and receive at least one approval before merging according to team policy. Before release, teams confirm that acceptance criteria are met, CI and scans are passing, release notes and rollback plans are prepared, and post-deployment verification is completed. After milestones, releases, or incidents, retrospectives capture lessons learned and convert them into tracked improvement actions.
